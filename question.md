# Question

*Agreed on 2026-11-09. This is the reference for the rest of the project.*

- **Question:** Is northern Italy turning into a single core around Milan, or did Turin and Genoa stall because of the industries they depended on? Or did the decline of those industries *trigger* the move towards Milan?

- **Theory or mechanism:** Krugman's (1991) core–periphery model (Lectures 7–8), in the "footloose entrepreneur" version (Forslid & Ottaviano 2003).
  - **Who moves:** graduates and knowledge workers. Each firm needs skilled people, so firms move with them.
  - **Who stays:** manufacturing workers whose skills fit one local industry (e.g. cars in Turin), plus the less mobile population. Their wages and spending keep demand in the periphery.
  - **Three possible answers:**
    - **A: Milan's pull** (the mechanism *inside* the model). Falling trade and communication costs start circular cumulative causation: graduates move to Milan, firms follow, Milan becomes more attractive and more graduates come.
    - **B: Industry decline** (a shock from *outside* the model). Cars, steel and shipbuilding shrank for global reasons. Turin and Genoa were hit because they depended on them, and would have stalled even without Milan.
    - **C: Both, in sequence** (the answer I find most realistic). The shock (B) weakened Turin and Genoa first; Milan's pull (A) then amplified it. The model says one city will become the core but not *which*; history, here the shock, decides it.

- **Concepts to keep separate:**
  - **Core lock-in** (Krugman): once a core forms, agglomeration rents keep it there. This is an agglomeration force and an *outcome* of the model.
  - **Skill lock-in:** workers are tied to their city by industry-specific skills. This is an *assumption* of the model and a dispersion force.
  - **Path dependence** appears in both B (Turin's past made it vulnerable) and A (once Milan is ahead, it tends to stay ahead).
  - **Outside the model:** production and headquarters moving *abroad* (Stellantis) leave the Milan–Turin system. This is part of shock B.

- **Expected pattern:** to be set out in detail in `framework.md`, before looking at the data.

- **Scope:**
  - **Places:** NUTS3 provinces of Milan, Turin, Genoa (the old triangle); Bologna, Verona, Padua (doing well, for comparison); Bergamo, Brescia, Monza-Brianza (around Milan).
  - **Period:** 2000 to the latest available year.
  - **Measures:** population, jobs by industry, GDP, graduates.
  - **Caveats:**
    - The results show patterns, not proof of cause.
    - Milan's GDP per head is inflated by commuters, so employment and population are safer measures.
    - Graduate data by province may be hard to find.
